# Devspace landing-page creative direction

## What Devspace is

Devspace helps people choose a direction in digital work, start at the right level, learn the underlying skills, practise them, and build work they can show. It spans web, mobile, backend, data, AI, product, marketing, content, design, cloud, and security.

It is not a course library or a streak game. The landing page should present Devspace as a practical launchpad for people who want to become capable and employable in the digital economy.

The emotional promise is:

> Find your direction. Build real capability. Make work that opens doors.

## Audience

Primary: ambitious beginners and early-career learners, especially people who may know they want a better digital career but do not yet know which path is theirs or what a credible first step looks like.

They need clarity, momentum, and evidence that the journey leads somewhere real. They do not need another mascot-led productivity loop or an over-promised AI tutor.

## Standard to beat

Avoid the familiar education-product formula: a friendly pastel gradient, a generic headline, floating UI cards, a smiling stock learner, then a row of feature icons.

Instead, the page should have the confidence and authorship of an ambitious product studio. It can be expressive, but every expressive decision must support the idea of making, momentum, or possibility.

## Reference set

### LiveLabs

Use as the benchmark for conviction and visual authorship. The oversized type, cropped imagery, near-black canvas, and art-directed collage make a clear world before the visitor reads a feature list.

Take: scale, restraint, editorial image treatment, and a direct point of view.

Do not take: the exact dark-green palette, collage execution, or studio-service posture. Devspace needs to feel participatory: a learner should see their own future in the page.

### Granola

Use as the benchmark for making the product itself the proof. Its page explains a concrete before/during/after workflow through real interface artifacts rather than abstract benefit cards.

Take: calm confidence, one memorable product promise, and real product surfaces used as evidence.

### Cloudflare

Found through Mobbin as a minimal technology reference. Use its lesson in functional credibility: firm hierarchy, direct copy, and a dense-but-orderly information system.

Take: clarity and confidence. Do not inherit enterprise visual heaviness.

### Butter, Harvey, Pangram, and Granola

These were shortlisted in Mobbin because they cross creative tools, serious AI, and type-led identity. They are useful for studying how a site can feel authored and contemporary without defaulting to the education category.

## Recommended design territory: The Launch Lab

Devspace should feel like a place where a future is being assembled. Think editorial technology journal meets modern creative tool: precise, energetic, tactile, and intelligently optimistic.

### Visual language

- A warm mineral or ink-black base, with Devspace blue as a deliberate signal rather than a page-wide wash.
- One headline display typeface with real personality, paired with a precise sans-serif for interface and proof.
- Large compositional type, not just a large headline. Type can establish rhythm, crop at the edge, or become a structural element.
- Original learner artifacts: a route map, a coded project brief, a portfolio tile, a skill passport, a path-selection moment. These are stronger than generic dashboard mockups.
- Devy appears sparingly as a guide within a system, never as the hero decoration.
- Motion should reveal progress and construction: paths connect, projects stack, a proof surface resolves. Avoid perpetual floating or ornamental blob motion.

### Hero hypothesis

Eyebrow: `YOUR NEXT CAREER IS BUILT, NOT BINGED.`

Headline: `Find the work you are meant to make.`

Supporting line: `Devspace turns a direction into a tailored path, real practice, and portfolio-worthy projects.`

Primary CTA: `Find my direction`

Secondary CTA: `See how a path works`

Hero visual: an art-directed "career launch board" that moves from interest and capability signals into a named route, visible skill blocks, and a first project. It must look like an active instrument, not a static course-card grid.

## Proof to earn trust

The first page should foreground real evidence instead of invented scale:

- A short, tangible route example: `Frontend developer → JavaScript foundations → interactive interface → portfolio project`.
- A real assessment or onboarding decision moment.
- A project artifact and the skills it demonstrates.
- Learner stories or outcomes once available; until then, do not fabricate jobs, completion counts, or partner logos.
- A transparent explanation of what Devy does: helps plan and unblock work; it does not replace learning or pretend to do the work for the learner.

## Page narrative

1. **The invitation** — a bold future-facing hero and one clear CTA.
2. **The problem with generic learning** — endless content does not tell people what to learn or what to make next.
3. **Your route becomes concrete** — show the direction-to-path-to-project sequence.
4. **Capability is visible** — show lessons and practice as building blocks, not isolated gamified tasks.
5. **Work that travels** — introduce projects, portfolio evidence, and practical outcomes.
6. **Devy as a useful guide** — explain the assistant in one disciplined, product-led section.
7. **A decisive close** — return to finding a direction, with no cluttered pricing wall or generic newsletter ask.

## Non-negotiables

- No generic education illustration style.
- No repeated gradient cards, floating glass panels, or decorative blobs.
- No fake social proof or empty stats.
- No claim that a streak, XP, or league is the value proposition.
- The visual world must be ownable enough that it could not be swapped onto Mimo, Duolingo, or Brilliant unchanged.

## Next design deliverable

Make a focused stylescape with three routes, then choose one before layout work:

1. **Launch Lab** — editorial type, warm dark base, luminous blue signals, project artifacts.
2. **Field Notes** — bright paper base, annotated diagrams, practical learning evidence, less cinematic.
3. **Signal System** — darker technical base, animated route graph, bolder motion and sharper product energy.

The recommended route is **Launch Lab**. It has the strongest chance of matching the expressive standard of LiveLabs while remaining specific to Devspace.
